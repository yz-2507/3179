Obesity

Overview

Obesity or being overweight is synonymous with having excessive body fat. It can be the root cause of various health problems. Obesity is defined as having a body mass index (BMI) over 30. The above choropleth map presents an overview of obesity across the globe. The box and whisker plot on the right gives a statistical summary of obesity rate in different continents.

Obesity rate in Australia

The line chart on the right shows how obesity rate changed from 1975 to 2016. In Australia, approximately 2 out of 3 adults are either obese or overweight. It is more prevalent amongst disadvantaged population.

Use the drop-down menu to see each line separately.

BMI and obesity rate

The bubble charts show the correlation between the mean BMI and obesity rate across different countries in 2016. Generally, countries with higher mean BMI would have higher obesity rate. For both male and female, the countries with the highest mean BMI and obesity rate are from Oceania. This is also evident in the above box and whisker plot.

Click the buttons to switch between bubble charts on male and female.

Obesity and life expectancy

This bubble chart compares obesity rate with life expectancy. Generally, higher obesity rate is associated with lower life expectancy. However, on a population scale, the correlation is not as strong, with some countries having lower obesity rate but also lower life expectancy and vice versa. This shows that whilst having higher obesity rate can reduce life expectancy, but there are many other factors which may affect life expectancy on a population scale.

Use the slider to filter by minimum population.
